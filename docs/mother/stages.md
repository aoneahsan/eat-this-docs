# Stages and daily needs

## Daily needs

Pregnancy needs are the same in all three trimesters. Values are for ages 19–30, from the
<a href="https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13050/chapter/24" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">National Academies Dietary Reference Intakes</a>.

| Nutrient | Pregnancy | Breastfeeding |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 71 g | 71 g |
| Fibre (AI) | 28 g | 29 g |
| Calcium | 1,000 mg | 1,000 mg |
| Iron | 27 mg | 9 mg |
| Magnesium | 350 mg | 310 mg |
| Zinc | 11 mg | 12 mg |
| Vitamin C | 85 mg | 120 mg |
| Vitamin B12 | 2.6 µg | 2.8 µg |
| Vitamin D | 15 µg | 15 µg |
| Vitamin A | 770 µg | 1,300 µg |
| Folate | 600 µg DFE | 500 µg DFE |
| Vitamin B6 | 1.9 mg | 2.0 mg |
| Alpha-linolenic acid, plant omega-3 (AI) | 1.4 g | 1.3 g |

**AI** marks an adequate intake. The other figures are recommended dietary allowances (RDA).

**Trying for a baby** has no separate figures. It uses the app's usual daily values, listed in
[Where the numbers come from](../sources.md).

## What each stage notes

### Trying for a baby

Foods ranked for folate, iron and vitamin B12.

- The NHS advises a daily folic acid tablet from before you start trying until week 12. Ask your pharmacist or doctor
  which one is right for you.
- Keep oily fish to 2 portions a week, and don't eat shark, swordfish or marlin.
- Avoiding alcohol is advised while you try.

Sources: <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/pregnancy-vitamins-and-supplements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, vitamins and supplements in pregnancy</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-types/fish-and-shellfish-nutrition/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, fish and shellfish</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/trying-for-a-baby/planning-your-pregnancy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, planning your pregnancy</a>

### First trimester (weeks 1–12)

- The NHS advises a daily folic acid tablet until week 12. Ask your midwife or pharmacist about it, and about vitamin D.
- Feeling sick is common early on. It usually clears up by weeks 16 to 20.
- You don't need to eat for two, even with twins or triplets.
- Caffeine: no more than 200 mg a day. A cup of tea, black or green, has about 75 mg; instant coffee about 100 mg.
- It's safest to avoid alcohol.
- Wash fruit, vegetables and salad well to remove soil.

Sources: <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/pregnancy-vitamins-and-supplements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, vitamins and supplements in pregnancy</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, vomiting and morning sickness</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/have-a-healthy-diet/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, a healthy diet in pregnancy</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, foods to avoid in pregnancy</a>

### Second trimester (weeks 13–27)

- Most people first feel their baby move between 16 and 24 weeks. If you haven't by 24 weeks, tell your midwife.
- Heartburn is more common from 12 weeks on. Smaller meals and nothing in the 3 hours before bed help.
- Two portions of fish a week, one of them oily, but no more than 2 portions of oily fish.
- Smoked fish such as smoked salmon should be cooked until steaming hot.

Sources: <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, your baby's movements</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/indigestion-and-heartburn/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, indigestion and heartburn</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/have-a-healthy-diet/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, a healthy diet in pregnancy</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, foods to avoid in pregnancy</a>

### Third trimester (weeks 28–42)

- Your iron is checked again around 28 weeks.
- Some swelling of feet and ankles is normal late on. Sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet needs checking
  straight away.
- A change in your baby's movements means calling your midwife or maternity unit now, even at night.

Sources: <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/pregnancy-vitamins-and-supplements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, vitamins and supplements in pregnancy</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/swollen-ankles-feet-and-fingers/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, swollen ankles, feet and fingers</a> · <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, your baby's movements</a>

### Breastfeeding

Foods ranked for calcium, protein and vitamin B12.

- You don't need a special diet. Keep a drink nearby while you feed: water, lower-fat milk or diluted fruit juice.
- Caffeine: no more than 200 mg a day. Cutting down matters most while your baby is under 6 months. A mug of green tea
  has about 38 mg.
- No more than 2 portions of oily fish a week. If you eat no animal foods, you may need vitamin B12 from foods or a
  supplement: ask your doctor.
- Ask your doctor or health visitor about vitamin D, especially in autumn and winter.

Source: <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/breastfeeding-and-lifestyle/diet/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">NHS, breastfeeding and diet</a>
