Feelings and conditions

Each page in the app checks warning signs before anything else. They come in three levels:

LevelWhat to do
EmergencyCall your local emergency number now. No foods are shown.
UrgentContact your midwife or doctor today. No foods are shown.
DoctorBook a visit. The foods are shown below the advice.

Food grades are explained in Evidence and safety.

Warning signs on every pregnancy page​

These are checked on every feeling and condition that covers pregnancy, alongside its own signs.

  • Emergency, call your local emergency number now. Severe tummy pain or heavy bleeding.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Your baby is moving less, has stopped moving, or the pattern has changed.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet, a severe headache, or blurred vision or flashing lights.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Sudden shortness of breath, or breathlessness with chest pain.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Bleeding from the vagina, or fluid leaking.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. A high temperature, or itchy skin.

Sources: NHS, pregnancy symptoms you need to get help for · NHS, stomach pain in pregnancy · NHS, your baby's movements

On breastfeeding pages this one is checked too:

  • Emergency, call your local emergency number now. You feel you can't keep yourself or someone else safe.

Source: NHS, where to get urgent help for mental health

Feeling sick or being sick​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. You can't keep food or fluids down for 24 hours, or your pee is very dark or you haven't peed for 8 hours.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. You're vomiting blood, have tummy pain or a high temperature, feel very weak or faint when you stand, or have lost weight.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Dry toast or a plain biscuit in the morning.
  • Small, frequent meals of plain foods.
  • Cold food if the smell of hot meals makes you feel sick.
  • Sip drinks little and often.

Foods

  • Ginger: strong evidence. The NHS suggests foods or drinks with ginger.
  • Brown rice: strong evidence. The NHS names bread and rice as plain, high-carbohydrate, low-fat foods.
  • Whole wheat flour: strong evidence. The NHS names bread and rice as plain, high-carbohydrate, low-fat foods.

Source: NHS, vomiting and morning sickness

Heartburn or indigestion​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Heartburn that doesn't go away with antacids, severe pain just below your ribs, or feeling very unwell.
  • Doctor, book a visit. Trouble swallowing, a hoarse voice, a cough that keeps coming back, swollen glands on both sides of your neck, pain or swelling in your stomach, or weight loss.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Smaller meals more often.
  • Nothing in the 3 hours before bed.
  • Sit upright while you eat and for a while after.
  • Less caffeine, and fewer rich, spicy or fatty foods.

Foods: none. No food is named for heartburn. How and when you eat makes the difference.

Sources: NHS, indigestion and heartburn in pregnancy · NHS, swollen ankles, feet and fingers in pregnancy

Constipation​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • None of its own.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Drink plenty of water with the extra fibre.

Foods

  • Prunes: strong evidence. High-fibre; dried fruit is an NHS pregnancy snack.
  • Dried figs: strong evidence. High-fibre dried fruit.
  • Red lentils: strong evidence. High-fibre pulse.
  • Chickpeas: strong evidence. High-fibre pulse.
  • Pear: strong evidence. Fruit with fibre.
  • Brown rice: strong evidence. Wholegrain, higher-fibre starch.

Sources: NHS, common health problems in pregnancy · NHS, have a healthy diet in pregnancy

Piles​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • None of its own.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Plenty of water.
  • Ask your doctor, midwife or pharmacist before using any cream or medicine for piles.

Foods

  • Whole wheat flour: strong evidence. Wholemeal bread or roti keeps stools soft.
  • Red lentils: strong evidence. High-fibre.
  • Apple: strong evidence. Fruit with fibre.

Sources: NHS, piles in pregnancy · NHS, common health problems in pregnancy

Tired all the time​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Doctor, book a visit. You can't sleep and also feel hopeless or have lost interest in things you enjoyed.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • No tea, coffee or cola in the evening.
  • Ask your midwife about an iron check if you're tired all the time.

Foods

  • Red lentils: strong evidence. Iron; tiredness can be low iron.
  • Spinach: strong evidence. Dark-green leafy vegetable with iron.
  • Dried apricots: strong evidence. Dried fruit with iron.

Sources: NHS, tiredness and sleep problems in pregnancy · NHS, iron deficiency anaemia

Low iron or anaemia (low red blood cells)​

For: Trying for a baby, pregnancy (all trimesters) and breastfeeding.

Warning signs

  • Doctor, book a visit. You think your iron may be low: tired, short of breath, a racing heart, or paler skin than usual.
  • Doctor, book a visit. Craving things that aren't food, such as paper or ice.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above and the breastfeeding sign above.

Tips

  • Large amounts of tea, coffee, milk and dairy make iron harder to absorb.
  • Liver is rich in iron but is not advised in pregnancy.
  • Your iron is checked at your booking appointment and around 28 weeks.
  • Food may not be enough on its own: your GP or midwife may recommend iron tablets.

Foods

  • Spinach: strong evidence. Dark-green leafy vegetable.
  • Kale: strong evidence. Dark-green leafy vegetable.
  • Mustard greens: strong evidence. Dark-green leafy vegetable.
  • Lean beef: strong evidence. Meat.
  • Goat meat: strong evidence. Meat.
  • Red lentils: strong evidence. Pulse.
  • Chickpeas: strong evidence. Pulse.
  • Kidney beans: strong evidence. Pulse.
  • Dried apricots: strong evidence. Dried fruit.
  • Raisins: strong evidence. Dried fruit.

Sources: NHS, iron deficiency anaemia · NHS, foods to avoid in pregnancy · NHS, vitamins, supplements and nutrition in pregnancy

Leg cramps​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • None of its own.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • A review of the trials found that magnesium supplements gave mixed results for cramps in pregnancy.
  • Regular gentle exercise, especially ankle and leg movements.

Foods: none. No food has good evidence for leg cramps in pregnancy. The NHS says regular gentle exercise, especially ankle and leg movements, may help prevent cramp.

Sources: Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2020, Interventions for leg cramps during pregnancy · NHS, common health problems in pregnancy

Back pain​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Emergency, call your local emergency number now. Back pain with loss of feeling in one or both legs, your bottom or your genitals.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Back pain in the second or third trimester (it can be early labour), with a fever, bleeding or pain when you pee, or pain in your side under your ribs.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Bend your knees and keep your back straight when you lift, and wear flat shoes.
  • A warm bath, a massage, or a back care class.

Foods: none. No food treats back pain. The app shows the foods for your stage, with the NHS self-care tips.

Source: NHS, back pain in pregnancy

Pelvic pain​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Doctor, book a visit. Moving is difficult, or getting out of a car, turning over in bed or using stairs hurts.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Stay active but stop what makes it worse; sit down to dress; a pillow between your legs in bed.

Foods: none. No food treats pelvic pain. A physiotherapist can help: ask your midwife.

Source: NHS, pelvic pain in pregnancy

Swollen or sore feet and ankles​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet, a severe headache, vision problems, severe pain just below your ribs, heartburn that antacids don't ease, feeling very unwell, or feeling sick and vomiting.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Feet up when you can; comfortable shoes; avoid standing for long.
  • Foot exercises: move each foot up and down 30 times, then circle it 8 times each way.

Foods: none. The NHS gives no food advice for swollen or sore feet. Gradual swelling late in pregnancy is common.

Source: NHS, swollen ankles, feet and fingers in pregnancy

Upset tummy or diarrhoea​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters) and breastfeeding.

Warning signs

  • Emergency, call your local emergency number now. Vomiting blood or vomit like coffee grounds, green vomit, a sudden severe tummy ache or headache, a stiff neck with pain in bright light, confusion, or severe trouble breathing.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Blood in your poo or bleeding from your bottom, or you can't keep fluids down.
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Vomiting or diarrhoea for more than 48 hours (pregnancy only).
  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. Diarrhoea for more than 7 days, or vomiting for more than 2 days (breastfeeding only).
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above and the breastfeeding sign above.

Tips

  • Small sips of fluids, often.
  • Eat when you feel able; avoid fatty or spicy foods.
  • Avoid fruit juice and fizzy drinks for now.
  • If you're pregnant, call your midwife, GP or your local health line for advice.
  • Keep breastfeeding your baby (breastfeeding only).

Foods: none. Fluids come first. No food is named for an upset tummy.

Sources: NHS, diarrhoea and vomiting · NHS, foods to avoid in pregnancy

Headache​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Urgent, contact your midwife or doctor today. A severe headache, blurred vision or flashing lights, pain just below your ribs, vomiting, or sudden swelling.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Drink plenty of fluids.

Foods: none. No food is named for headaches in pregnancy. Fluids help.

Source: NHS, headaches in pregnancy

Low mood or anxious​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters) and breastfeeding.

Warning signs

  • Emergency, call your local emergency number now. You don't feel you can keep yourself or someone else safe.
  • Doctor, book a visit. Stress, anxiety or low mood that affects your daily life, or you're worried about your mental health.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above and the breastfeeding sign above.

Tips

  • A healthy, balanced diet, and telling your midwife or doctor how you really feel.

Foods: none. No food treats low mood. Talk to your midwife or doctor about how you feel.

Sources: NHS, where to get urgent help for mental health · NHS, pregnancy symptoms you need to get help for · NHS, mental health in pregnancy

Can't sleep​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Doctor, book a visit. You can't sleep and also feel hopeless or have lost interest in things.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • No tea, coffee or cola in the evening.

Foods: none. No food is named for sleep in pregnancy. Less caffeine later in the day helps.

Source: NHS, tiredness and sleep problems in pregnancy

Gestational diabetes​

For: Pregnancy (all trimesters).

Warning signs

  • Doctor, book a visit. Very thirsty, peeing more, a dry mouth, tiredness, blurred eyesight, or genital itching or thrush.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Changing what you eat and being more active may lower blood sugar. Ask your midwife or doctor about a food plan.

Foods: none. Ask your midwife or doctor about food for gestational diabetes, so Eat This shows no picks here.

Source: NHS, gestational diabetes

The last weeks (36 weeks on)​

For: Third trimester.

Warning signs

  • None of its own.
  • Plus the pregnancy signs above.

Tips

  • Keep eating a varied diet; you don't need extra food.

Foods

  • Dates: some evidence. Small trials linked eating dates in the last weeks with a shorter early or active phase of labour; the trials were low to moderate quality.

Sources: NHS, have a healthy diet in pregnancy · BMC Pregnancy Childbirth 2020, Effects of date fruit on labor and delivery outcomes: a systematic review and meta-analysis · Explore (NY) 2021, The effect of late pregnancy date fruit consumption on delivery progress: a meta-analysis
