What each stage notes​

Trying for a baby​

Foods ranked for folate, iron and vitamin B12.

  • The NHS advises a daily folic acid tablet from before you start trying until week 12. Ask your pharmacist or doctor which one is right for you.
  • Keep oily fish to 2 portions a week, and don't eat shark, swordfish or marlin.
  • Avoiding alcohol is advised while you try.

Sources: NHS, vitamins and supplements in pregnancy · NHS, fish and shellfish · NHS, planning your pregnancy

First trimester (weeks 1–12)​

  • The NHS advises a daily folic acid tablet until week 12. Ask your midwife or pharmacist about it, and about vitamin D.
  • Feeling sick is common early on. It usually clears up by weeks 16 to 20.
  • You don't need to eat for two, even with twins or triplets.
  • Caffeine: no more than 200 mg a day. A cup of tea, black or green, has about 75 mg; instant coffee about 100 mg.
  • It's safest to avoid alcohol.
  • Wash fruit, vegetables and salad well to remove soil.

Sources: NHS, vitamins and supplements in pregnancy · NHS, vomiting and morning sickness · NHS, a healthy diet in pregnancy · NHS, foods to avoid in pregnancy

Second trimester (weeks 13–27)​

  • Most people first feel their baby move between 16 and 24 weeks. If you haven't by 24 weeks, tell your midwife.
  • Heartburn is more common from 12 weeks on. Smaller meals and nothing in the 3 hours before bed help.
  • Two portions of fish a week, one of them oily, but no more than 2 portions of oily fish.
  • Smoked fish such as smoked salmon should be cooked until steaming hot.

Sources: NHS, your baby's movements · NHS, indigestion and heartburn · NHS, a healthy diet in pregnancy · NHS, foods to avoid in pregnancy

Third trimester (weeks 28–42)​

  • Your iron is checked again around 28 weeks.
  • Some swelling of feet and ankles is normal late on. Sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet needs checking straight away.
  • A change in your baby's movements means calling your midwife or maternity unit now, even at night.

Sources: NHS, vitamins and supplements in pregnancy · NHS, swollen ankles, feet and fingers · NHS, your baby's movements

Breastfeeding​

Foods ranked for calcium, protein and vitamin B12.

  • You don't need a special diet. Keep a drink nearby while you feed: water, lower-fat milk or diluted fruit juice.
  • Caffeine: no more than 200 mg a day. Cutting down matters most while your baby is under 6 months. A mug of green tea has about 38 mg.
  • No more than 2 portions of oily fish a week. If you eat no animal foods, you may need vitamin B12 from foods or a supplement: ask your doctor.
  • Ask your doctor or health visitor about vitamin D, especially in autumn and winter.

Source: NHS, breastfeeding and diet
